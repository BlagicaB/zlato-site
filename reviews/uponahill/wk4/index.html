Wed · Aug 5 Artist · Fun fact Facebook + Instagram

Elizabeth Moen didn't play her first open mic until she was 20

Artist posts perform, but only when there's a hook. "Acclaimed singer-songwriter" is wallpaper. "She was a French and Spanish major who taught herself guitar and didn't get on a stage until senior year" is a story. Tag her so it has a chance of being reshared — that's what took the July 26 Liam Kazar post to 2,800 views.

Facebook

Elizabeth Moen opens Up On A Hill on September 2 — and she got a very late start. She taught herself guitar as a teenager in small-town Iowa but didn't play her first open mic until she was 20, as a French and Spanish major at the University of Iowa, at a bar in Iowa City called The Mill. She'd just written her first song. Nine years later she's toured Europe singing in Kevin Morby's band, shared stages with Brittany Howard, Hozier and Ani DiFranco, and had NPR say she "has remarkable control over her voice that reflects the emotional candor in her lyrics." Her new record was produced by Spencer Tweedy. She's releasing it herself. She describes what she does as indie rock grit through the warmth of Midwestern folk. In a tent on a hill at sunset, with 200 people, that's going to be something. September 2. Gates at 4. Tickets at uponahill.org

Sun · Aug 9 Music · The why Facebook + Instagram

This lineup is not a coincidence

The strongest thing about the bill is that these people already know each other. It proves Erik Hall curated rather than booked — and it tags five accounts at once, which is five chances at the kind of reshare that carried the Liam Kazar post to 2,800 views.

Facebook

Look closely at this lineup and you'll notice it's less a bill than a circle of friends. Quin Kirchner — the DJ who opens all three nights — has played drums in Erik Hall's bands. Erik is the musician who curated this festival, and the one who recorded Steve Reich's "Music for 18 Musicians" by himself, in his own home studio, playing every single part by hand. Steve Reich told him he'd reinvented the piece. Spencer Tweedy produced Elizabeth Moen's new record. He also played drums on Liam Kazar's. Liam has been in Jeff Tweedy's band for over a decade. Big Sadie has been playing Chicago rooms since 2014, and one of them teaches fiddle at the Old Town School of Folk Music. This is what it looks like when a musician books a festival instead of an agent. Everyone on this hill in September has played on someone else's record. September 2, 16 and 30. Event 2 Wed · Sept 16 Big Sadie

Up On A Hill: Big Sadie

The middle night, and the one with the deepest local roots — Big Sadie are a Chicago band, which makes them the closest thing on the bill to a hometown act for the drive-over-from-Illinois audience.

Event description

The second night of Up On A Hill — three evenings of music on a private hillside in Harbor Country, with every ticket supporting three Southwest Michigan nonprofits. THE ARTIST Big Sadie is a Chicago roots band: upright bass, guitar, fiddle and close harmony, playing original country songs alongside bluegrass and old-time music that sounds a century old. At the center are Elise Bergman and Collin Moore — a married pair who've been playing together since 2005, she a Chicago native out of the city's blues and folk scene, he raised in East Tennessee learning music from his mother. They formed the band in 2014. A few things that tell you who they are: when WTTW needed a band to talk about Ken Burns's country music documentary and Chicago's forgotten place in that history, they called Big Sadie. Bloodshot Records — the label that more or less defined Chicago's roots scene — put them on "Too Late to Pray: Defiant Chicago Roots." And one of them teaches fiddle at the Old Town School of Folk Music, which in Chicago is a kind of credential. If you've ever wanted to hear three voices lock together in a tent on a hill at sunset, this is your night. THE EVENING 4:00 pm — Gates open. Event 3 Wed · Sept 30 Liam Kazar

Up On A Hill: Liam Kazar

Our best-performing artist so far by a wide margin — the July 26 post hit 2,800 views on Instagram against 54 on Facebook, on identical copy. Something in his orbit carried it. This event is written to be worth resharing.

Event description

The final night of the inaugural Up On A Hill — three evenings of music on a private hillside in Harbor Country, with every ticket supporting three Southwest Michigan nonprofits. THE ARTIST Liam Kazar writes warm, groove-forward songs somewhere between folk, soul and classic pop — the kind of record that sounds like a Sunday and a Saturday night at the same time. Jeff Tweedy, who would know, says: "Whenever I hear one of his songs for the first time I almost immediately start thinking… I love this song." Kazar has been a working musician since he was 17, when he played SXSW with the Chicago band Kids These Days. He's spent over a decade in Jeff Tweedy's band and has backed Steve Gunn, Chance the Rapper and Daniel Johnston. His sister is Sima Cunningham of OHMME. His latest album, "Pilot Light," was produced by Sam Evian in the Catskills. And when the pandemic took away both touring and his bartending job, he started cooking his mother's Armenian recipes for a living — a project he named Isfahan, after a city in Iran with one of the world's oldest Armenian quarters, and after his favorite Duke Ellington tune. He'll tell you he grew up watching Food Network instead of cartoons. THE EVENING 4:00 pm — Gates open. Free parking on site. 4:30 pm — DJ Quin Kirchner, a Chicago jazz drummer playing deep cuts off his own vinyl. 6:00 pm — Liam Kazar. THE PLACE Apple Blossom Resort in New Buffalo, Michigan — a private 18-acre estate in the heart of Harbor Country, just over an hour from downtown Chicago and minutes from Three Oaks and the lakeshore.
